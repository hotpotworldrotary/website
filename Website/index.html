How to enjoy BBQNướng

1

Pick 2 meats

Choose from fifteen cuts: beef brisket, rib eye, rib meat and short rib, pork shoulder, thick pork belly, chicken thigh, lamb and head-on prawns, plus marinated beef, pork and chicken.

2

Order from your server

BBQ meats are ordered two at a time. When you are ready for more, order your next two.

3

Grill and repeat

Cook it at your table and keep going. Sauces are at the sauce bar: soy, hoisin, sriracha, chili oil, garlic, sesame oil, green onion and peanut sauce.

House rules

  • 90 minute dining time, if applicable.
  • BBQ meats are ordered from your server, 2 at a time.
  • No take-out.
  • $15 charge for excessive leftovers. Please order responsibly to help reduce food waste.

Things worth knowing before you sit down.

Everyone gets their own pot. You do not have to negotiate a broth with the rest of your table. Order the one you want.

Thin meat cooks fast. Sliced beef and lamb need a few seconds in a rolling boil, not a few minutes. Dumplings, root vegetables and corn take longer.

Take it slow. There is no rush and no set course order. Cook a little, eat it, go back for more.

Tell us about allergies and diets. Because you pick every ingredient yourself, hot pot is one of the easiest meals to eat around a restriction. Vegetarians can order the vegetable broth and stay entirely in the vegetable, tofu, mushroom and noodle plates.

Our story

Dinner cooked at the table, not in a kitchen.

Hot pot is an East Asian way of eating: a simmering pot of stock set right on your table, and a spread of raw ingredients you cook in it yourself, a few pieces at a time.

There are three parts to it. The broth you cook in. The ingredients you dip. The sauce you dip them into afterwards. Everything else is up to you.

It is also, quietly, one of the healthier ways to eat out. Nothing is deep fried or drowned in sauce before it reaches you. It is vegetables, mushrooms, seafood and thin cut meat, cooked in broth, seasoned by you.

We did not pick one country.

Most hot pot restaurants commit to a single tradition and cook within it. We did the opposite. Our broths come from across Asia: Vietnam, China, Thailand, Korea and beyond, sitting on the same menu.

Then there is the belt. A conveyor runs around the dining room carrying vegetables, noodles and dumplings under clear lids, so you see them before you take them.
